Abstract
HiT-UNet · Visual
HiT-UNet: Hierarchical Temporal U-Net with Shot-Aware Transformers for Supervised Video Summarization
Saadman Sakib
1Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET), Chattogram-4349, Bangladesh
Correspondence: Saadman Sakib (saadmansakib110442@gmail.com)
Keywords: Video summarization  |  HiT-UNet  |  Temporal U-Net  |  Hierarchical transformer  |  Shot-aware attention  |  ViT encoder

Video summarization aims to generate a compact video skim that preserves the most informative content while avoiding redundancy. Although recent transformer-based methods improve long-range temporal modeling, many operate at a single temporal scale or ignore shot-level structure, limiting temporal coherence and generalization. We propose HiT-UNet, a supervised video summarization framework that integrates a pretrained ViT as a feature-level temporal encoder, a multi-scale 1D Temporal U-Net backbone for hierarchical temporal modeling, and a hierarchical shot-aware transformer scoring head to capture inter-shot context for refined frame importance prediction. Frame-level scores are aggregated into shot utilities under a fixed-length budget, while training employs a weighted focal loss to address severe class imbalance. Under the canonical protocol, HiT-UNet achieves F1-scores of 65.8% on SumMe and 79.92% on TVSum, outperforming prior work while maintaining strong diversity (64.98% and 48.68%).

MM-HiT-UNet · Vision-Language
Multimodal Video Summarization Using Vision-Language Embeddings and Hierarchical Temporal Modeling
Saadman Sakib  ·  Kaushik Deb
1Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET), Chattogram-4349, Bangladesh
Correspondence: Kaushik Deb (debkaushik99@cuet.ac.bd)
Keywords: Video summarization  |  Multimodal fusion  |  CLIP  |  BLIP-2  |  Temporal U-Net  |  Hierarchical transformer  |  Vision-language models

Video summarization aims to produce concise representations of videos by selecting the most informative frames or shots. Existing methods predominantly rely on unimodal visual features extracted from convolutional neural networks, overlooking the rich semantic information that natural language can provide. In this paper, we present an integrated multimodal video summarization framework that makes two tightly coupled contributions. Initially, we construct a semantically enriched multimodal dataset by augmenting standard video summarization benchmarks (SumMe, TVSum, OVP, and YouTube) with BLIP-2-generated frame-level captions and CLIP-based aligned visual–textual embeddings, producing four feature variants—Image-only, Text-only, Averaged fusion, and Concatenated fusion—for systematic analysis of modality contributions. We validate the dataset through cross-modal retrieval, similarity gap analysis, Spearman complementarity correlation, and caption quality diagnostics, confirming that the generated captions are semantically meaningful and that the two modalities provide complementary information (average Spearman ρ ≈ 0.53). Finally, we develop a multi-scale temporal summarization architecture comprising a U-Net-based temporal backbone and a hierarchical transformer-style scoring head. Our best configuration achieves F1-scores of 59.27% on SumMe, competitive with recent state-of-the-art methods while maintaining only 28.27M parameters with linear computational scaling.

Method

Pipeline

The HiT-UNet pipeline runs in five stages and remains the default for quick, visual-only summarization.

  1. 1. Features. GoogLeNet pool5 activations (1024-d, L2-normalized) on every 15th frame.
  2. 2. Shot detection. KTS cpd_auto segments the sequence into shots.
  3. 3. Encoder. Per-frame features are projected to ViT dimensionality and processed by pretrained ViT-Base blocks with sinusoidal position encodings.
  4. 4. Backbone. A 3-level Temporal U-Net with intra-level self-attention captures multi-scale temporal context.
  5. 5. Head. Shot-level and frame-level transformers refine per-frame importance; a knapsack solver respects the 20% duration budget.

MM-HiT-UNet replaces the visual-only feature with a vision-language feature. GoogLeNet is still used, but only to drive KTS (the paper's GoogLeNet-CPS protocol).

  1. 1. Captioning. BLIP-2 (opt-2.7b) generates a short caption per sampled frame.
  2. 2. Multimodal feature. CLIP ViT-B/32 encodes image (512-d) and caption (512-d). Both are L2-normalised and concatenated to a 1024-d vector.
  3. 3. Segmentation. GoogLeNet pool5 + KTS yields the shot boundaries (per-paper GoogLeNet-CPS protocol).
  4. 4. Backbone. 2-level U-Net, 192 base channels, dropout 0.50. The ViT encoder is disabled here — per the ablation, pretrained image ViT introduces a domain mismatch on CLIP features.
  5. 5. Head. 2-head hierarchical transformer, 3 shot-level + 3 frame-level layers, FF dim 1024, dropout 0.12. Same knapsack at 20%.

First Part 2 request downloads BLIP-2 (~12 GB) from HuggingFace. It's cached from then on.
